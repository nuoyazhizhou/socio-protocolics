## S1 | University of Paris 1231 Papal Bull: Rule Text, Not Implementation Effect

- **Source identity and positioning**: Pope Gregory IX, *Parens scientiarum* (1231). This round uses the [Italian translation and editor's notes](https://www.rm.unina.it/didattica/fonti/frova/sez7/par1_stampa.htm) compiled by Carla Frova in *Istruzione e educazione nel Medioevo* VII.1. That page notes the base manuscript is Denifle's *Chartularium Universitatis Parisiensis* I (1889), pp.136–139, no.79. This round did not collate the Latin base manuscript word by word.
- **Actual reading**: the full translation text on the web page and the editor's notes; key locating of the chancellor's licensing procedure beginning "Tutti coloro", the regulatory power beginning "Inoltre, poiché", the conditional suspension beginning "E se per caso" and the following paragraph, and the teaching restrictions beginning "I maestri nelle arti" and the following paragraph.
- **Document provisions**: partial internal regulations and conditional suspension power coexist with the chancellor's licensing procedure and partial teaching restrictions.
- **Editor's interpretation**: the page's introduction places the document in the context of the 1229 conflict and suspension, and evaluates it as limiting the chancellor's power. This is the editor's narrative, and this problem has not independently checked the relevant original events.
- **This problem's mechanism interpretation**: recognition by a higher authority can simultaneously contain protection and control, and should be encoded item by item; it cannot be simply recorded as "fully independent" or "fully absorbed".
- **Gap**: actual use of the rights, implementation constraints, long-term resources and knowledge output. The text does not by itself establish an autonomy effect or the overall European path.

## S2 | Donglin and Other Private Academies: Reverse Constraint in Second-Hand Research

- **Source identity**: Benjamin A. Elman (1989), *Imperial Politics and Confucian Societies in Late Imperial China: The Hanlin and Donglin Academies*, *Modern China* 15(4):379–418. [Author-institution hosted paper](https://swh.princeton.edu/~elman/documents/Imperial_Politics_and_Confucian_Societies.pdf).
- **Actual reading**: this round focused on checking the printed pp.387–388 "Confucian Private Academies" paragraph and p.389 examination-channel passage; PDF pp.9–11 (zero-indexed, including cover). No claim of having read through or checked the full historical materials.
- **Researcher's interpretation**: the said paragraphs discuss the private academies' claim to relative educational autonomy, and the tension between moral/philosophical education and exam-training; p.389 also discusses how political claims were transmitted via exam success and entry into the bureaucracy. The two observations can coexist; one cannot automatically equate political access with full convergence of all teaching activities.
- **This problem's use**: opposes presetting "all academies are exam pre-processors" as fact; it is not an autonomy-effect estimate newly obtained by this problem.
- **Gap**: one must return to the specific-period statutes, funding, personnel, coursework and teaching records, distinguishing educational goals, self-reports and actual resource allocation. This partial material proves neither that all academies are independent nor that they hold parallel certification power.

## S3 | Institutional Isomorphism: Existing Explanations That Must Be Seriously Compared

- **Source identity**: Paul J. DiMaggio and Walter W. Powell (1983), *The Iron Cage Revisited: Institutional Isomorphism and Collective Rationality in Organizational Fields*, *American Sociological Review* 48(2):147–160. [Author-website paper](https://woodypowell.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/DiMaggioPowellThe-Iron-Cage-Revisited1983.pdf).
- **Actual reading**: focused on checking the printed pp.154–155 "Predictors of Isomorphic Change", especially A-1, A-2, A-3; PDF pp.8–9 (zero-indexed, including cover).
- **Original paper's claim**: dependence, concentrated resource supply and means–ends uncertainty correspond to different conditional convergence predictions; the authors emphasise other things being equal and propose subsequent empirical tests, and cannot be described as only doing categorical narrative.
- **This problem's obligation**: first check whether certification returns and organisational convergence are already covered by these mechanisms; adding new terminology does not count as an original prediction. The possible increment is to separately test the specific rights configuration and different autonomy outcomes, but it is not yet proven to have an increment.
- **Gap**: a systematic literature search of elite co-optation, resource dependence, historical educational institutions and organisational autonomy is not yet complete; one must not claim this problem originated certification competition or is superior to the relevant disciplines.
