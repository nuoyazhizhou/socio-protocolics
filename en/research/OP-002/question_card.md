# OP-002 | Certification Systems and Organisational Autonomy

Version: open question card v0.1 | Date: 2026-10-27 | Chinese original; English translation pending.

Status: **candidate mechanism and research entry; a partial source initial-check exists, historical comparison, causal identification or effect experiment are not completed, and no pre-registration.** OP-002 is a research-catalogue number, not a new RFC.

**Question.** When an organisation needs external qualifications, resources or protection, under what conditions will it invest more resources into accessing an existing certification system? When does access transfer the organisation's control rights, and when does it instead protect internal autonomy and independent exploration?

**Why it is worth asking.** Shift from "why did a certain historical path not happen" to checking the concrete relation among certification returns, authorisation clauses, resource allocation and organisational autonomy. Historical results are known; this problem is first and foremost a retrospective mechanism study; it does not pre-set "China co-opted, Europe autonomous", nor pre-set that a common standard causes a decline in exploration diversity.

**What we propose.** [Research Notes](research_notes.md) registers four candidate empirical hypotheses — H1—H4 — each separately supportable, rejectable or narrowable: certification returns and input, authorisation and control rights, protective authorisation, and certification arrangements and exploration space. They are cross-group research questions newly added by the author, not existing theorems of the whitepaper, nor is it proven that they are better than existing explanations such as institutional isomorphism.

**Existing material.** [Source and Boundary](source_and_boundary.md) lists the translation of the 1231 University of Paris papal bull and its base-manuscript location, partial passages of the Donglin Academy study, and DiMaggio and Powell's existing conditional predictions. In the bull, protection and restriction coexist; the academies' claim to relative educational autonomy also means "fully exam-ised" cannot be a case-selection premise. Stipulation and claim alike do not equal actual effect.

**How one may take it over.** One may complete only one of the following, without first agreeing with protocolics, and without requiring mathematics, simulation and cross-national research all at once:

- Check one charter or franchise document, distinguishing who grants qualifications, who decides curriculum and personnel, and who can revoke decisions.
- Add one "rule—action—result" source chain for a historical or contemporary organisation, retaining same-case contrary observations.
- Compare existing explanations, pointing out that this problem is only a restatement, or proposing observations that can truly distinguish explanations.
- Build a local model containing choices of access, independence, mixing and exit, and check whether convergence depends on artificial settings.
- Separately compare whether the engineering representation helps analysts reduce omissions; this tool study does not substitute for historical-mechanism testing.

**First submittable result.** A located "authorisation clause — actual control right — implementation evidence — unknown" comparison table; one organisation alone may be checked. With no behavioural data, submit a rule-material table, clearly stating that autonomy change cannot yet be judged.

**How to return to the whitepaper.** Dock with [RFC-011 (authority model and social bridging)](../../whitepaper/chapter-05.md#sp-rfc-011), [RFC-014 (institutional instantiation and normative boundary)](../../whitepaper/chapter-05.md#sp-rfc-014), and [RFC-018 (evolutionary screening)](../../whitepaper/chapter-07.md#sp-rfc-018) of v11.2. Access, control-right transfer, content convergence and exploration change are adjudicated separately; one paper does not vouch for the whole chain or the whole whitepaper. Evidence is currently insufficient, **so the core clauses are not revised for now**.
