# H02 | How formal status affects adoption

Status: material research draft v0.1; not pre-registered, no complete corpus built, and one cannot claim on this basis to have identified a causal effect.

## Question and minimal task

In [*The Historical Conception of Literature* (《历史的文学观念论》)](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/歷史的文學觀念論), Hu Shi links canonical literary status with whether an author commits fully. We want to check: did contemporaneous actors understand it the same way? Could an increase in adoption have preceded formal recognition?

The first step selects about five obtainable contemporaneous texts from around 1917–1920, records supporting and opposing opinions, and distinguishes advocacy, expectation, self-reported behaviour and observable behaviour. The time range may be adjusted for material availability, but the reason for adjustment must be recorded, and one cannot select the results first and then call the range pre-specified.

## Data specification and availability

Fields: document title, author, date, publication/volume/issue/page, scan or transcription link, version, genre, original sentence, whether formal status is asserted, expected benefit or risk, whether an actual adoption record exists, alternative explanation, coder and disagreements.

The two Hu Shi originals already have public transcriptions; other contemporaneous materials need to be confirmed one by one for access rights and scan quality. The complete count, missingness pattern and representativeness are currently unknown. Submitting the first batch of coding tables already constitutes a useful contribution, and one is not required to obtain database access first.

## How to adjudicate

The conditions for passing the material trial are: citations are locatable, advocacy and behaviour are separated, and counterexamples are not excluded. Finding only "someone thinks status affects commitment" supports the actor's mechanistic judgement, not that the effect has occurred.

To identify an effect later, one needs an independently defined adoption outcome — for example the vernacular proportion of new works in a designated publication, an author's sustained submissions, or textbook use — with the denominator recorded, and comparisons of explanations such as publication opportunity, reader market, political mobilisation and educational change. One cannot synthesise literary canonicity, classroom access and administrative recognition into the same binary variable.

If adoption had kept growing before recognition, lacks relevant incentive statements, or market and organisational change better explain the difference, the scope of "formal status drives adoption" should be limited; these pieces of evidence do not automatically overturn all expected mechanisms, nor can they be interpreted as supporting this framework under any circumstances.

## What the author can provide

Coding-table structure, cross-version organisation and engineering support; readers with expertise in intellectual history and sociolinguistics are needed to help supplement material and correct interpretations. The output can first stand as a checkable material set, without pre-committing to a paper or co-authorship.
