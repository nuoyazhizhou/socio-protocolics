# OP-001 | Engineering-Oriented Checklist: Can It Reduce Errors in Intermediary Analysis?

Status: **open design draft v0.2; not pre-registered, not recruited, no experiment conducted.**

Date: 2026-10-23 | Chinese original; English translation pending.

**Question.** Under the same information, cases and usage time, can checklist B — organised with middleware, authority, bypass and version — help adult readers with an engineering background compare the retention, deletion and substitution of an intermediary more accurately than self-written cost-and-process checklist A?

**Why it is worth asking.** Popular readings claim that an engineering perspective helps understand collaboration. This help should be tested, and cannot be substituted by readers finding it novel, a formula running, or existing audit studies. That the intermediary may lower coordination cost is already researched and is not a new finding of this card.

**Ready-made inputs.** [Three public fictional cases, the A/B checklists, the unified answer format and reference answers](material_pack.md). The public problems are for material review and pre-testing, and cannot be used to establish the superiority of B. For provenance and inheritance see [source verification](source_and_boundary.md).

**Minimal connectable work.** Check the case conditions and the fairness of the two sets of material, and submit concrete revisions with reasons; this single item may be completed first, without committing to the whole empirical study. If the pre-test continues, a small number of voluntary target readers may be used to find wording problems; for example, a total of 8–12 people is only a feasibility starting point, not the sample size for an effect test.

**Subsequent comparison.** Use brand-new unpublished cases, between-participant random assignment, identical information, tools and time; externally review the cases and reference answers, keep scoring as blind to assignment as possible, and retain scoring disagreements. A is not the overall representative of existing social-science theory, and B winning does not establish a new discipline.

**How to judge.** The primary indicator is the personal mean of reading scores (0–10 points) on unseen problems, then normalised. Δ = B − A; the candidate minimum meaningful gain is 0.10. The formal study first locks the version, sample size and interval algorithm: only when the lower bound of the 95% interval reaches 0.10 is that local gain supported; if the upper bound is below 0.10, that target is not supported; if the interval contains 0.10 but the lower bound does not reach it, it cannot yet be adjudicated. Time spent, term misunderstandings and boundary errors must be reported in full; one cannot show only the average score. Details are per [Research Notes](research_notes.md).

**What results are welcome.** Material errors, already-existing equivalent research, zero gain, negative gain, and evidence that engineering vocabulary makes readers more prone to misjudge. The correct answers of the software worked examples hold only within the scenario, and are not measured true values of social net contribution.

**What is still missing.** Systematic search of checklist/human-factors research, a formal unseen question bank, external-material review, sample-size planning and real-reader data. The author-side existing contribution is text, material and an engineering-oriented specification; collaboration arrangements are agreed separately according to actual contribution.

Connection to the popular chapter: [Did Removing Two Approvals Really Reduce the Work?](../../../articles/intermediary/删掉两道审批_工作真的少了吗.md). This card only tests one analytical arrangement, and does not establish the validity of the six core proposition groups, social isomorphism, or Socio-Radar.
