# OP-001: Does the Engineering-Oriented Arrangement of Intermediary Net-Contribution Analysis Help Readers?

Version: design draft v0.2 | Date: 2026-10-23 | Status: not pre-registered, not recruited, not experimented, no effect data obtained. Chinese original; English translation pending.

## 1. Research Question and the Identity of the Contribution

Under the same process material, information fields and reading conditions, can an analysis checklist organised with engineering vocabulary — middleware, authority, bypass, version — help adult readers with an engineering background compare the retention, deletion and substitution of an intermediary more accurately?

This is a candidate study about the **usability of expression and analysis methods**. It does not present "the intermediary may have a net benefit" as a new finding, does not establish that society and computing are isomorphic, and does not test the universality of the six core proposition groups. Even if the result is positive, it only supports the local gain of this engineering-oriented arrangement relative to a specified control.

It uses the P1/P2 background and draws on the master source's O4, the applicability boundary of RFC-004, the to-be-tested risk of RFC-005, the normative objective of RFC-007, and the migration-accounting boundary of RFC-017; it does not alter the original attribution of the three postulates — the six core proposition groups — the 19 effective SP-RFC items (of which 007 is a normative rule). [Master source](../../whitepaper/chapter-01.md)

## 3. Goals, Boundaries and Observable Quantities

### 3.1 Process-Level Comparison

For each case, first determine the same demand population, observation period, rule version and acceptance requirement. Candidate schemes are denoted:

- **D**: no studied intermediary function is set up; the two ends process directly;
- **M**: retain the existing intermediary arrangement;
- **M′**: an alternative arrangement that must explicitly state which functions are retained, which work is transferred, and what new cost is added.

An M′ that is "without intermediary" may retain automatic validation, back-office staff or a complaint channel, and cannot be recorded as D merely because the name changed. Each case must state who has the authority to make the adjustment, and whether the original security, service accessibility and review conditions are maintained.

Record the following quantities, **without synthesising a social-health total score**:

| Quantity | Unit and meaning | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Total labour input | person-minutes of processing, rework, support and migration by all relevant actors within a fixed population and period | the same time must not be double-counted across two categories; recording only internal institutions misses applicant burden |
| Waiting and completion latency | calendar minutes/hours per demand item | cannot be added directly to person-minutes |
| Service quality | count and denominator of items qualified by the ex-ante task standard and completed on time | initial errors, final completions and on-time completions are recorded separately |
| Coverage | count of attempts, registrations, completions and dropouts | not registered does not equal no demand, nor does it directly equal effective demand |
| Cost and allocation | payment, payee, price unit and external burden | a payment transfer does not automatically equal an equal amount of social-resource loss |
| Authority and correction | who can interpret, reject, change rules, who can exit and review | first describe verifiable facts; rent-seeking cannot be concluded merely from centralisation or high price |

When different quantities cannot be uniformly converted, report the vector of costs and results and the constraints; do not compress rights, latency and cost into an apparently objective "net value" through arbitrary weights.

Net contribution is not defined by whether M exists. One should compare the independent cost and results of feasible schemes. A function not triggered in the short term is insufficient to conclude that it has no recovery or tail-risk benefit; conversely, one cannot use an unverifiable "there might be a disaster" to indefinitely exempt any gate.

### 3.2 Reader-Level Comparison

The primary indicator is participants' **reading score** on unseen cases, 0–10 points per case, normalised to [0,1] by taking the personal mean and dividing by 10. The scoring rubric must be frozen before grouping; the five dimensions respectively concern boundary, calculation, quality and coverage, scheme choice, and unknown information. The rubric is in the material pack.

Secondary indicators include completion time, engineering-term misunderstandings, the count of unsupported assertions that "one more layer must be bad", and the count of mistaking a payment transfer for a resource cost or a registration denominator for the whole demand. A self-rated "very enlightening" is only a subjective experience and does not substitute for correctness.

## 4. Minimal Execution Unit: Material Pre-Test First

This pack already provides three public fictional case cards, the A/B checklists, a unified answer format and conditional reference answers. The public problems are only for checking whether the material is understandable and whether the answers are ambiguous, **not for proving the superiority of B**.

The successor's first deliverable may contain only:

1. Case by case, confirm whether the numbers, units, schemes and constraints are sufficient, and point out at least one missing condition that could change the answer; if none, state that none was found, and do not manufacture problems.
2. Check whether the two checklists are fair in information, length and training time; revise both sides simultaneously, preserving the version difference.
3. Ask a small number of target readers to trial-read, for example a total of 8–12 voluntary adults. This size is only a feasible pre-test for discovering wording problems, **not the sample size required for an effect test**; the first two items can be completed first even with no participants.
4. Deliver de-identified answers, a misunderstanding list and a revision record. If researchers actually recruit and use answers as research, they should first confirm applicable informed-consent, privacy and institutional requirements; this round performed no recruitment.

One must not let readers first see the popular sample chapter and its answers, then use their answer accuracy as evidence that the framework is valid. A post-reading comprehension test may be done separately, but it must be named separately and list its research question separately.

## 5. Candidate Design for Subsequent Controlled Comparison

After the pre-test, use **brand-new, unpublished and unused-for-training cases** for a two-arm comparison. The cases should include productive intermediaries, redundant steps, mixed functions and insufficient-evidence situations; do not quota by political stance, nor write only the question types B is best at.

- The target population is tentatively limited to adult readers with process, testing, operations or development experience. One must not on this basis infer the general public or social scientists.
- Use between-participant random assignment; stratify by pre-registered engineering background. Each person uses only A or B, to avoid migration learning from the same person switching.
- Both arms receive the same problem text, information and answer format, and the same training, answering time and available tools. Keep the checklist character-count difference within 10%; close character counts do not equal equal cognitive load, and the pre-test must still check this.
- Both A and B contain all key questions; do not deliberately simplify A into "only counting layers", nor provide either arm extra answers, case explanations or author-prestige hints.
- The formal cases and conditional reference answers are first reviewed by domain/method reviewers other than the problem author. For judgements with essential disputes, list the allowable answers and reasons; do not force a unique gold standard.
- Scorers should as far as possible not know the assignment; if the raw answers expose terminology, report that blinding may have failed. At least two people score independently and retain initial results and disagreement adjudication; do not report only the post-adjudication perfect agreement.

**Not yet in place**: a new formal question bank, external-material review, a formal sample size and a pre-registration record. Therefore this file is a connectable design draft, not a complete qualification proof that "effective empirical work can be conducted now".

## 6. Presumed Gain and Possible Failure

Let Δ be the difference between the average normalised reading scores of arm B and arm A. The candidate minimum meaningful gain takes **δ=0.10**, i.e. an average increase of 1 point on a 10-point scale. This is a pragmatic threshold tentatively set by this design, not a social constant or a value derived from theory; if adjusted after the pre-test, a new version must be published first, then tested with new confirmatory data.

Before formal execution, lock the sample size, stratification method, missing-data and exclusion rules, question-bank version and 95% confidence-interval algorithm. The sample size must be planned from δ, score variation and test requirements; the 8–12-person pre-test cannot be directly upgraded to validation. The analysis takes the participant as the randomisation unit, and does not treat multiple questions from the same person as independent samples; the limited nature of the question sample is reported separately.

| Pre-defined interval result | What may be said |
|---|---|
| The lower bound of Δ's 95% interval reaches 0.10, and no unexplained serious material/scoring bias exists | supports achieving the preset score gain in this population, task and A/B version; duration and error types must still be reported in full |
| The upper bound of Δ's 95% interval is below 0.10 | this comparison does not support the preset minimum meaningful gain, and one cannot continue to claim that the target has been reached |
| The interval contains 0.10 but the lower bound is below 0.10 | the evidence cannot yet adjudicate the preset gain; do not use statistical non-significance to impersonate no effect |

If B adds unsupported "parasitic/deadlock" judgements, clearly increases time, or wins only on questions intentionally tailored to the framework, then even if the average rises, these costs must be reported and not summarised as "overall better".

If only the results of the same fixed set of questions are reported, the conclusion applies only to those questions; to claim generalisation to other cases requires adding question sources, sampling and reproduction. If changing terminology shows no change in performance, it may also mean plain language was already sufficient, and one should not further protect the framework by adding "good intermediary/bad intermediary".

## 7. Data and Delivery Format

The minimum data record is as follows; do not collect real names, affiliations, political attitudes or real-case material:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| participant_id | string | randomised anonymous identifier |
| phase | enum | pilot / confirmatory; must not be mixed |
| arm, background_stratum | enum | assignment, background layer recorded before assignment |
| case_id, case_version, checklist_version | string | problem and intervention material bound together |
| answer, missing_information | text | original answer, supplementation needed; not filled in by the researcher |
| duration_seconds | non-negative or missing | completion time under the same timing rule; interruptions recorded separately |
| rater_id, rubric_version, scores_initial | identifier and integer vector | each rater's initial five 0–2 scores |
| adjudicated_scores, adjudication_reason | vector and text | retain the adjudication basis, do not overwrite the initial scores |
| exclusion_reason | enum or empty | decided by pre-set rules; cannot exclude for answering wrongly |

The successor returns versioned material, anonymous data, scoring disagreements, actual analysis and unsupported results. Answers not cleared for open release are not published; field descriptions and summary results may be shared first. LLM-generated answers must be labelled separately and must not be passed off as human samples.

## 9. v0.2 Supplementary Execution Boundary

This round only organised materials and checked the external-research-related passages; no recruitment, random assignment, scoring or effect estimation was performed. The Zhejiang institutional documents and existing foreign empirical studies are separately in the [real-case table](../cases/II-CN-A_housing_fund_registration_disposition.md) and the [foreign-research check](../cases/II-INT_audit_and_driving_license_research_check.md); they do not enter the numerical values of the three fictional problems, nor are they this experiment's human-answering data.

The formal study must also pre-handle non-response, dropout, timeout, non-compliance with the checklist, repeated participation and material leakage; report the primary analysis by assigned arm, and cannot retain only completers or those who understand engineering terms. Missing scores are not automatically zero; the specific missing-data handling must be set first and its sensitivity reported. External raters must check whether scoring favours the author's concepts; equivalent plain-language expressions found should receive the same score.

This pack distinguishes two questions: first, how the intermediary affects the real process; second, whether the engineering-oriented expression improves reader judgement. OP-001 directly tests the second question; even if the score rises, one cannot on this basis claim that RFC-005's complexity–rent-seeking feedback is established. The control in the public problems is a cost-and-process checklist, not the whole of institutional economics.
