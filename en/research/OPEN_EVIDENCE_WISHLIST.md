## 1. Why Do We Expose Empirical Gaps?

*Socio-Protocolics* strictly enforces **Red Line 8 (Epistemological Prudence & Empirical Boundaries)** and **Red Line 9 (Real Cases First & Counter-Factual Testing)**. Across all 8 chapters and appendices, we refuse to repackage conjectures as universal physical laws, or present secondary citations as verified primary sources.

This wishlist outlines the **four major tiers of hard evidence** most urgently needed from peer specialists and the wider academic community. Scholars, archivists, or developers who provide verified primary sources, high-resolution scans, or reproducible econometric code will receive **permanent academic attribution and acknowledgments** in the whitepaper's footnotes, revision logs, and contributor registry.

## 2. Evidence Grading Standard

Submitted materials are audited and graded by our editorial review team according to the following criteria:

| Evidence Tier | Criteria & Acceptable Proofs | Applicability in Whitepaper |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier A (Primary Verification)** | High-resolution scans/photocopies of archival originals (official gazettes, rare local gazetteers, imperial memorials), unabridged journal articles from authoritative databases (JSTOR, ScienceDirect, Oxford Academic) with exact page numbers, raw tables from official statistical yearbooks, or fully reproducible open-source econometric scripts and datasets. | Serves as load-bearing empirical evidence, resolves internal audit defects, and directly substantiates core propositions. |
| **Tier B (Authoritative Secondary)** | Exact volume and section citations from punctuated authoritative editions (e.g., Zhonghua Book Company, Commercial Press), explicit citations from top peer-reviewed journals (*QJE*, *AER*, *JPE*, *Economic History Review*), or established dynastic histories. | Serves as supporting background evidence and secondary confirmation. |
| **Tier C (Candidate Lead)** | Academic memoirs, journalistic accounts, open web leads, or working papers from non-refereed institutions. | Recorded as working leads; does not bear independent theoretical proof. |

## 4. How to Claim a Task & Submit Contributions

Contributing to the empirical foundation of *Socio-Protocolics* follows standard open-source GitHub workflows:

### 1. Claiming a Task
- Navigate to the GitHub Issues tab and create a new Issue with the title:  
  `[Task Claim] Task ID: e.g., W-HIST-01 Ministry of Education Gazette Scan`
- Briefly describe your search channels or materials on hand to prevent duplicate efforts.

### 2. Submitting Materials (Pull Request)
- Place high-resolution scan PDFs, text citation excerpts, or econometric scripts into the corresponding directory under `research/evidence_inbox/`;
- Open a Pull Request accompanied by an **Evidence Verification Memo**:
  ```markdown
  ### Task ID: W-HIST-01
  - Document Title: Education Gazette, Year 7, Issue 2, Official Documents, p. 12
  - Provenance: National Library of China Periodicals Database (with accession number & resolution)
  - Key Excerpt: "Ordered that primary schools in 1st and 2nd grades replace classical text with vernacular..."
  - Suggested Edit to Whitepaper: Confirmed directive number in §5.4 is accurate; suggest appending high-res scan to Appendix Evidence Cards.
  ```

### 3. Review & Acknowledgment
- The editorial review team will conduct an independent source audit within 3 business days;
- Upon passing verification, your contribution is merged into the master branch and permanently credited:
  1. Direct footnote citation in the corresponding chapter of the whitepaper: *"Special thanks to [Contributor Name / GitHub ID] for retrieving and verifying this archival document."*
  2. Recorded in the repository's public `CONTRIBUTING.md` Contributor Hall of Fame;
  3. The task on this wishlist will be marked **🟢 Resolved** with the closing justification link.
