## Core Questions and Dependencies

When irreversible investment faces a rule change, does the change come from performance probability, recoverable amount, expected revenue, cost, financing or demand? Distinguish first, then compare; one cannot call all non-investment "credibility collapse".

This group takes P2/P3 as the main modelling background, with clauses 008/009/010; the chapter end separately lists the non-numbered consumption and policy application note. The static conditional relation in Whitepaper §4.1 does not automatically identify a real effect: only when recovery from breach is lower than the gain, with other items fixed, does a fall in performance probability reduce that item's net present value; exiting also requires crossing a threshold.

## Success/Failure Dimensions, Comparison and Counter-Evidence

Record, per project, the rule version, original commitment, duration, revenue / cost, breach recovery, relief, financing conditions, and the application / bid / win / finance / build / commission status and denominator. Legal stability, expectation stability and actual performance are coded separately; adding new support does not automatically equal retroactively destroying old rights.

Mechanism comparison includes at least credible commitment, real option, project profitability, financing constraint, market demand and supply cost. First make the contract and timeline; if comparable projects near a phased or threshold point are obtained, then assess the identification conditions. One cannot take the industry's total investment or stock price alone as the performance-belief.

Counter-evidence directions: the rule is stable yet no one invests because of insufficient gain / cost; risk rises but investment increases because of compensation or higher gain; the actual rule differs from the rumour; adoption precedes the so-called credibility change. AR5 is especially used to constrain the erroneous extrapolation "long-term commitment necessarily brings investment", not to predetermine that it shows the credibility mechanism failed.
