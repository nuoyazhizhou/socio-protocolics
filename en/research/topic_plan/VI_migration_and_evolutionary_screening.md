## Core Questions and Dependencies

How do retention, replacement, phasing and bypass compare under the same boundary and lifecycle? Does multi-branch only thin out the budget? Can a dashboard's usage or predicted benefit represent true service effect?

This group follows the main background of P1/P2 and 017/018; 019 retains the application / research norm entry, not a VI-group deductive inference. One cannot write non-freely-migratable as technical-debt conservation, nor treat one go-live record as a sure win for gradual refactoring. 017 is an accounting framework; 018's probability relation has conditions such as independence and budget; 019's proxy and threshold are not yet calibrated.

The four cases differ in service type, data sensitivity, organisation and period, and only a structured comparison is done first. Content migration and identity verification in particular cannot be directly ranked into a unified success/failure list; what is commonly studied is how cost, legacy function, exit and evidence are recorded.

## Success/Failure Dimensions, Comparison and Counter-Evidence

The cost dictionary must at least separate old maintenance, one-time migration, new maintenance, service stoppage, retry, training, user burden, data / right protection and invisible assets; person-hours, calendar time, cost and loss are kept separate. Net present value is computed only after unifying the period, price and discounting caliber; unknowns are not filled with zero.

Mechanism comparison includes project scope, complexity, supplier incentive, procurement, data quality, resources, optimism bias, migration and compatibility. First compare scheme feasibility; do not use a project with no actually feasible bypass as proof of "failure because bypass was not adopted".

Counter-evidence directions: complete replacement has higher net benefit at the same quality; incremental migration is more expensive because of long-term dual-track; retaining old patches is instead continuously harmful; multi-branch jointly fails, crowds out resources or gives feedback too early; usage growth but no qualified-service improvement. All of these should enter the judgement, rather than being renamed "ineffective evolution" and pushed aside.
