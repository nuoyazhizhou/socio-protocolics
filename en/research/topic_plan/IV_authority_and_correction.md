## Core Questions and Dependencies

Distinguish knowing the risk, raising an objection, having the right to pause, actually pausing, reviewing and executing the correction. Multiple departments do not automatically constitute split-brain; disagreement does not automatically mean malice; zero penalty does not automatically imply exploitative dominance.

This group follows P1/P2/P3 and 011/012/013/014/015. The static game requires a complete payoff comparison; group dynamics, information failure and physical accidents are each argued separately. Ring-0/3 is a limited-authority analogy and cannot substitute for statutory authority or an organisation's real decision chain.

## Success/Failure Dimensions, Comparison and Counter-Evidence

Record separately whether the decision is lawful / compliant, whether the objection reached, whether a pause right exists and is usable, whether correction was executed, and the damage and recovery. Rescue success can coexist with the original mission's failure; reconsideration acceptance or revocation does not equal relief realised.

Compare explanations such as clarity of responsibility, information filtering, technical uncertainty, risk exposure, insufficient resources, incentive, supervision and relief. Build item by item a time-stamped chain of "information — receiver — authority — decision — execution"; mark the unknown as unknown, and do not substitute hindsight common sense for the knowledge at the time.

Observations that weaken the authority explanation include: failure persists despite clear authority and an objection channel; information fully arrived but the technical model was wrong; correction existed but was not implemented; the so-called conflict is actually a legitimate division of labour. If independent payoffs and alternative actions cannot be defined, stop using 013's strict-dominance conclusion.
