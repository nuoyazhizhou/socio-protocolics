# H03 | What happens between rule promulgation and actual migration

Status: historical material trial v0.1; not pre-registered, no nationwide implementation-rate data.

## Minimal task

Check a 1920 local official document that forwarded a Ministry of Education directive, then look for two primary-school textbooks or usage records from before and after the reform, and fill in "what the rule stipulates, what the textbook changes, and how much actual adoption is known". The goal is to complete a small sample within one to two weeks; if material cannot be found, the search scope and gaps should still be left on record.

The known input is [vol. 1, no. 7 (1920) of the *Beijing School Administration Bureau Educational Administration Monthly*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6e/NLC404-01J002135-24988_%E4%BA%AC%E5%B8%AB%E5%AD%B8%E5%8B%99%E5%B1%80%E6%95%99%E8%82%B2%E8%A1%8C%E6%94%BF%E6%9C%88%E5%88%8A_1920%E5%B9%B41%E5%8D%B77%E6%9C%9F.pdf#page=8), scanned page 8, printed page "Orders" page 2. The local directive is dated 23 January 1920, citing "Ministry of Education Directive No. 12" (determined as the twelfth directive from the OCR transcription of the contemporaneous scan and the Ministry's directive numbering system). Here one must not treat the local forwarding date as the central ministry directive date.

## Data specification

Official document: issuing body, number, issuance/forwarding date, applicable region, school type, grade, course, effective time, original sentence, scanned page and printed page. Textbook: title, compiler, publisher, edition, year, applicable grade, table of contents, sampled pages, classical–vernacular mixing judgement and usage evidence.

Textbook candidates have not yet been obtained one by one, and one does not claim that a ready-made before-after comparison dataset exists. Publishing a textbook does not equal actual school use; single-school use does not equal nationwide implementation either.

## Judgement and possibly-overturned claims

As long as one shows that a certain change was phased by grade, that old textbooks remained, that supporting teacher training or local differences existed, that is sufficient to oppose the claim that "a single order switched the entire national education system"; one cannot assert on the basis of missing material alone that no implementation took place.

To explain migration speed further, one needs to specify the measurement object and start-end points, and compare textbook supply, teacher capability, funding, local execution and family demand. Rule-first or practice-first can both occur, and one cannot presuppose that all reforms are "bottom-up followed by retrospective ratification".

The author can provide field templates, version organisation and data checks; readers with expertise in education history are needed to check textbooks and the administrative system. The trial counts as successful when it accurately defines the evidence scope, not when it validates the whitepaper.
