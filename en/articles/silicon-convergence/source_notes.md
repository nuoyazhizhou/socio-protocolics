## 1. Core Literature and Empirical Datasets

### 1.1. Datacenter Energetics, GPU Duty Cycles, and Compute Economics
* **Core Fact**: Accelerators (e.g., NVIDIA H100 / B200) draw 700W~1200W per card, with datacenter PUE hovering around 1.2~1.5. Extended test-time reasoning consumes tangible grid power, cooling resources, and accelerated silicon depreciation, driving commercial AI providers to deploy usage caps and tiered metering.
* **Citations**:
  * Barroso, L. A., Hölzle, U., & Ranganathan, P. (2018). *The Datacenter as a Computer: Designing Warehouse-Scale Machines* (3rd ed.). Morgan & Claypool Publishers.
  * Patterson, D. A., & Hennessy, J. L. (2018). *Computer Organization and Design: The Hardware/Software Interface (RISC-V Edition)*. Morgan Kaufmann.
  * SemiAnalysis (2023/2024). *The Economics of Large Language Models: Training and Inference Cost Models*.

### 1.2. Mechanical Gear Fatigue, Actuators, and Battery Aging
* **Core Fact**: Precision transmission components (harmonic and planetary reducers) experience shear stresses governed by S-N fatigue curves and lack self-healing properties; lithium-ion batteries at high discharge rates and temperature extremes suffer SEI layer growth and dendrite formation, degrading state of health (SOH).
* **Citations**:
  * Stephens, R. I., Fatemi, A., Stephens, R. R., & Fuchs, H. O. (2000). *Metal Fatigue in Engineering* (2nd ed.). John Wiley & Sons.
  * Vetter, J., Novák, P., Wagner, M. R., Veit, C., Möller, K. C., Besenhard, J. O., ... & Hammouche, A. (2005). Ageing mechanisms in lithium-ion batteries. *Journal of Power Sources*, 147(1-2), 269–281.
  * Arora, S., et al. (2016). A review on machinery diagnostics and prognostics implementing condition-based maintenance. *Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing*, 76, 485–513.

### 1.3. Moravec's Paradox and Hostile Industrial Worksites
* **Core Fact**: Abstract symbolic logic requires trivial energy and compute, whereas micro-tactile perception, dynamic balance, and physical resilience require astronomical compute and fragile sensors; in penstocks and boilers, optical sensors and RF links fail rapidly.
* **Citations**:
  * Moravec, Hans (1988). *Mind Children: The Future of Robot and Human Intelligence*. Harvard University Press.
  * Raibert, Marc (1986). *Legged Robots That Balance*. MIT Press.
  * Cutkosky, M. R. (1989). On grasp choice, grasp models, and the design of hands for manufacturing tasks. *IEEE Transactions on Robotics and Automation*, 5(3), 269–279.

### 1.4. Comparative Advantage and Labor Substitution Frontiers
* **Core Fact**: Even when one entity maintains absolute productivity advantages across all domains, differing relative opportunity costs and wear expectations dictate that hazardous physical maintenance is subcontracted to lower marginal cost labor; automation Codifies routine tasks while non-routine manual work persists.
* **Citations**:
  * Ricardo, David (1817). *On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation*. London: John Murray.
  * Autor, David H. (2015). Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation. *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 29(3), 3–30.
  * Acemoglu, D., & Restrepo, P. (2018). The Race between Man and Machine: Implications of Technology for Growth, Factor Shares, and Employment. *American Economic Review*, 108(6), 1488–1542.

### 1.5. Skinner Box Conditioning and Algorithmic Labor Dispatch
* **Core Fact**: Variable-ratio reinforcement schedules produce higher persistence and resistance to extinction than fixed schedules; platform algorithms deploy pseudo-random surge pricing to condition driver work patterns into self-disciplined loops.
* **Citations**:
  * Skinner, B. F. (1953). *Science and Human Behavior*. Macmillan.
  * Zuboff, Shoshana (2019). *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight for a Human Future at the New Frontier of Power*. PublicAffairs.
