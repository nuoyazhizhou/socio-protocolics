## 2. Real Counter-Evidence, Competing Explanations, and Fallacy Screening

### 1. Real Counter-Evidence and Competing Explanations

| Dimension | Core Argument & Basis | How It Constrains This Essay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Same-Domain Counter-Example: High-Trust, High-Visibility Systems with Minimal ACKs** | Hospital trauma surgical rooms, automated assembly lines (Kanban/Poka-Yoke), firefighting crews: team members rarely exchange verbose confirmation messages, relying on pre-configured roles, SOPs, and direct physical visibility. | **Directly refutes the naive claim that "more feedback is always better"**: forces our analysis to recognize that **explicit feedback incurs non-zero costs**; when role architecture and physical interlocking are strong, implicit coordination outperforms check-in bureaucracy, demanding "Minimal Viable Feedback." |
| **Competing Explanation A: Lack of Psychological Safety** | Edmondson (1999) on team psychological safety: in punitive corporate cultures, asking clarifying questions is penalized as incompetence, leading employees to defensively reply "Received" and stay silent. | Fully acknowledged and incorporated; protocolics models this as a rational risk-averse Nash equilibrium under [SP-POSTULATE-2 (Strategic Agents)](../../whitepaper/chapter-01.md#sec-1-5). If a system rewards ceremonial compliance and punishes early risk signaling, it converges on performative ACKs. |
| **Competing Explanation B: Attention Overload & Notification Fatigue** | Employees face dozens of work groups and endless @channel alerts, exhausting finite cognitive bandwidth ([SP-POSTULATE-1](../../whitepaper/chapter-01.md#sec-1-5)). | "Received" degrades into a low-power keep-alive heartbeat to signal device presence, uncoupled from substantive comprehension. Constrains management protocols to restrict broadcast alerts. |
