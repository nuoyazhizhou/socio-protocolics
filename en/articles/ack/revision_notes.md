2. **Replacing Moralizing with the Six-Tier State Machine and Game Theory (Audit Correction 1)**:
   - Firmly rejected conventional management tropes that moralize communication failures as "poor execution" or "lack of employee responsibility";
   - Grounded the analysis in the Internet transport standard **RFC 9293 (TCP)** and systems engineering's classic **End-to-End Argument (Saltzer et al. 1984)**, constructing a **Six-Tier Task State Machine** from "L1 Transport Delivery" to "L6 Audit Closure";
   - Used the game-theoretic models of Darley & Latané (1968) and Olson (1965) to deduce that under unassigned multicast broadcasting, replying "Received" while taking zero action is the mathematically inevitable Nash dominant strategy.
3. **Introducing Meyer & Rowan's Institutional Sociology to Define "Performative ACKs" (Audit Correction 2)**:
   - Drew on institutional sociology pioneers Meyer & Rowan (1977) (*Formal Structure as Myth and Ceremony*) to analyze the structural incentives that drive organizations to decouple ceremonial compliance from technical production;
   - Strictly enforced the Whitepaper `SP-RFC-016` metric rule: in computing effective collaborative bandwidth, textual confirmations unbacked by verifiable resource or state handover carry an effective bandwidth weight of zero ($B_{\text{TCP}} = 0$).
4. **Solidifying Authentic Public Health Crisis Closed-Loop Archives (Audit Correction 3)**:
   - Systematically audited WHO primary archival records, elevating public health case references from generalized assertions into locatable primary evidence:
     - **2003 SARS Outbreak (WHO Update 79)**: Captured the internal "two-sided reality" of the same official bulletin—highly commending peak mobilization achievements while objectively noting persistent gaps in steady-state surveillance and data sharing;

     - **2015 WHO West Africa Interim Assessment (WHO 2015-07-07 Response)**: Documented the systemic execution decoupling that occurs when top-down alerts lack field-level operational supply pipelines and tracing frameworks.
5. **Adding Same-Domain Counter-Examples to Guard Against "Feedback Inflation" (Audit Correction 4)**:
   - Highlighted the acute danger of over-correction, where mandating check-ins at every micro-step triggers "Feedback Inflation" and congestion collapse;
   - Introduced hospital surgical theaters, automated automotive assembly lines, and firefighting crews as real-world counter-examples where **high reliability is achieved with minimal messaging**, explaining how role architecture and physical interlocking drastically reduce explicit coordination costs, and establishing the principle of "Minimal Viable Feedback."
