## 2. Real Counter-Evidence, Competing Explanations, and Excluded Unverified Claims

### 1. Real Counter-Evidence and Competing Explanations

| Explanation or Counter-Observation | Core Argument & Basis | How It Constrains This Essay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Same-Domain Neighbor Counter-Example: Long-Term Stability of the 1956 First-Round Simplification** | The 1956 *Chinese Character Simplification Scheme* and 1964 *General Table* also contained many-to-one homophone merges (e.g., `后/後`, `发/發/髮`, `干/乾/幹`), yet stabilized as the modern national standard rather than being repealed. | **Directly refutes any absolute claim that "all many-to-one merges inevitably fail"**: forces our analysis to distinguish **(a) full sentence context vs. restricted short fields**, **(b) long-standing folk simplifications with low collision density vs. aggressive high-density homophone merges**, and **(c) cold-start expansion vs. installed-base lock-in (`SP-RFC-017`)**. |
| **Competing Explanation A: Political & Institutional Environment Transition** | The Dec 1977 draft took shape in a specific historical period without broad scholarly consensus or cautious piloting; after 1978, national priorities shifted toward restoring educational and publishing order, emphasizing regulatory stability and procedural rigor. | Fully acknowledged as the macro-institutional backdrop; our protocolics analysis of disambiguation cost shifting and dictionary/ROM-chip migration burdens provides a **complementary technical and informational mechanism**, not an exclusive replacement for political history. |
| **Competing Explanation B: Visual Familiarity & Exposure Effect** | Reader resistance in 1977–1978 may have stemmed largely from low initial exposure to unfamiliar glyph shapes rather than steady-state informational entropy defects. | **Constrains the empirical verdict on `SP-RFC-001`**: without controlled experiments matching learning exposure, 1977–1978 reader friction cannot be attributed 100% to steady-state decoding cost; "initial familiarity shock" itself belongs to transition switching costs under `SP-RFC-017`. |
