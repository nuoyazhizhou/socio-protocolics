# From 3 Postulates, 6 Core Proposition Groups to 19 Numbered SP-RFC Items: Article and Research Entry Points

Version: Index v0.8 | Date: 2026-10-26 | Source language: Chinese. This is the English counterpart (en-v0.1); the Chinese index remains authoritative.

This is a writing and research navigation aid; it does not set up a separate set of definitions. Names, attribution and conditions are governed by [Whitepaper §1.5](../社会系统协议学白皮书.md#-04-协议学核心对象模型与公理体系layer-2-theoretical-primitive-spec), and the RFC links lead into the main body text. The chain 3→6→19 denotes modelling dependency and clause attribution; it does not imply that all the propositions have been rigorously demonstrated to follow from the three postulates.

## I. How the Three Tiers Connect to the Articles

- The 3 postulates provide the modelling starting point. P1 checks resource and cost boundaries; P2 preserves agents' autonomous objectives; P3 points to the connection between rules, beliefs, incentives and action. Articles list only the dependencies they actually rely on, and citing a postulate must not be presented as verification of that postulate.
- The 6 core proposition groups organise distinct questions. Each article selects one main topic and a small number of cross-cutting topics; it does not cram every case into language reform or protocol revision.
- The 19 numbered SP-RFC items provide concrete analytical entry points: 007 is the cross-group normative rule, 003 is a semantic application, and 019 is the Chapter 8 tooling/research norm; the former 006/010 were reassigned as non-numbered materials, with their research tasks retained. Each article must state the specific part it uses, and a mechanism must not be claimed on the strength of a keyword alone.

A particular caution: using P3 to discuss recognition and expectation does not imply that the third group's intertemporal performance model has been used; likewise, discussing a trial implementation does not imply that 018's independent branch-probability model has been tested.

The table below is the writing and research queue. Groups I–VI have each produced [concrete candidates and task plans](../research/主题计划/README.md); Group II additionally has a conceptual sample chapter and OP-001 materials, which have not passed the real-case acceptance stage (T3), and for which no effect data from this project exist and no full cross-case comparison has been carried out. Subsequent work follows the [case-first rule](../docs/案例驱动写作与命题检验.md) and writes out the target sub-claim, evidence, counter-material, adjudication and theoretical write-back. There is no mechanical requirement that each RFC be padded out to four articles, nor does the fact that one article touches several items imply that each of them has independent evidence.

## III. The Precise Coverage of the Hu Shi Case

The case uses objects O1/O2/O3/O4; postulates P1/P2 serve as background, while P3 organises the recognition–expectation–action mechanism that remains to be tested. The main topics are 001 in Group I and 014 in Group IV; 007 states the normative objective, and 017/018 are only qualitative extensions.

004, 005 (including the merged institutionalisation scenario), the candidate process diagram (non-numbered material) and 006's intermediary mechanism have not been tested in this article; Groups III and V have not been developed; and 011, 012, 013, 015 and 019, among others, cannot be counted as covered either. The full [item-by-item citation note](../articles/hu-shih/来源说明.md#与白皮书的对应关系) and the [article endnotes](../articles/hu-shih/如何评价胡适的作品.md#article-framework) set out the actual use and its limits.

Therefore, "cited in an article" in this index indicates only that a traceable use exists; it does not imply that the core proposition, its group or the relevant RFC have received empirical verification.

## V. Citation Conventions for Each Article

The main text's endnotes keep a readable summary: modelling background, main group and clause, normative objective, qualitative extension, untested parts and research entry points. The source note then lists, item by item, "number — position in the main text — role — boundary". External literature and framework citations are kept separate: historical sources support historical facts, while the whitepaper supplies the author's analytical language; mutual links do not constitute self-certification.

Local empirical support is discussed only where conditions, measurement, comparison and actual results are attached. Definitions, formal derivations, empirical mechanisms and normative choices each carry their own obligations; the RFCs cannot all be uniformly listed as theorems already borne out by stories.

This page uses the v11.2 numbering. The independent main-text section of each clause is consistent with the [version comparison](../docs/v11.2_编号与章节对照.md); the same number in an older version does not automatically denote the same clause.
